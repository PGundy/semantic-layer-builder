# Phase card: interview, one batch at a time

Load this card when `sl next` names phase `interview`. It holds the per-table interview, writing each answer with a typed command, the readbacks and the review form. Load `references/interview-guide.md` with it.

**Checklist:** 1. `sl status --json` for `next_batch`. 2. Per table: ask, write the answers, check joins (`phases/joins.md`), `sl readback`, `sl reply` with the owner's answer, `sl approve` on a recorded yes. 3. `sl validate` and `sl status` after the batch.

Take the tables in `next_batch` from `sl status --json` (`batch_size` tables, most important first). Owner priorities override it: order by the topics the owner put first and use `next_batch` only to fill a batch, with `sl status --source <s> --json` for the source the owner put first. For each table:

1. Ask what one row is (the baseline's grain guess, with its reason), then which of its candidate measures and splits the business uses, then go column by column only where the profile raises a question: status values, test data, units, which date counts, every `pii` and `sensitive` tag (and `public` for a field everyone may see although it looks sensitive), and for every time dimension which time zone "a day" means and, when the column has no time zone, which zone its values are recorded in (`time_zone`; the conversion per type and dialect is in `interview-guide.md`).
2. Write each answer into `models/<source>/<model>.yaml` immediately, with a typed edit: `description`, `grain`, `primary_key`, `filter`, `synonyms` (only ones the owner heard in a readback) and `time_zone` with `sl set <s>.<m> <key> <value>` or `sl set <s>.<m>.<field> <key> <value>`, `tags` with `sl tag <s>.<m>.<field> pii` (`--remove` takes one off), `open_questions` with `sl question add <s>.<m> --text "<question>"` and, once the owner answers one, `sl question resolve <s>.<m> --match "<question text>" --text "<the owner's answer>" --by "<owner>"`. `sl add-field` adds a field the draft left out and `sl rename` renames one with every reference to it. A rule that depends on a related table (test accounts flagged on customers, refunds of counted orders) is a join reference, `<join>.<column>` or two hops `<join>.<join>.<column>` (`order.customer.is_test_account` on refunds), through many_to_one or one_to_one joins; never a subquery or another table's name (`sl check-rule` tests one before you write it). Such a join is part of the field's definition: it applies whatever the role may see, without row filters. When the owner agrees a rule for one measure, ask in the same round trip whether it applies to the model's other measures and to related models' measures. See the guide.
3. Check and read back any join a join reference uses (`phases/joins.md`) before reading back that measure or dimension. Read each measure and dimension back, ending with "Approve?"; record the answer with `sl reply`, and on a yes, `sl approve --target <source>.<model>.<field>`.
4. When the grain, key and description are agreed, read the table back. The readback states who will see the table once it is approved, and does not ask: who sees what is decided once, for the whole layer, in the access phase (`phases/access.md`). When the owner wants that changed, record it in their words with `sl reply --readback <id> --item <n> --text "<their words>" --verdict change --about access --by "<owner>"`: it does not hold the table, and `sl next` brings it up in the access phase. While `access.yaml` is being rewritten, the readback says who will see the table under the draft file once it is approved. Their answer to the table itself is its own `sl reply`, and on a yes, `sl approve --target <source>.<model>`.

A known caveat on an approved item (a period known to be wrong, a segment not covered) goes into that item's own `description`. When the owner offers a trusted figure, write a draft golden for it right away (`phases/verify.md`), even before its measure exists, and `sl goldens run` it during the interview. Label a figure you computed by hand with a rule the layer cannot yet express as hand-computed, with the layer's own figure beside it, and describe the blocker as the missing column or view. A rule that needs an aggregate, a closure or a pre-aggregated table (a customer's first counted order) needs a table or view the owner's engineers create, on every dialect: on DuckDB, a view in the `.duckdb` file or a Parquet file the owner writes, read as one table expression, since `connection.views` holds no aggregate (`references/intake.md`); record an open question with the exact definition and figure. Ask engineering for a column or view only when no supported construct (a join reference within two hops, or a filter that writes the rule out) can express the rule; when one can, write the rule with it, and where the CLI still falls short (a warning that does not fit, a check it cannot run), wait for the tool and say so, and write in the field's `description` why its warning stands.

**Removing a drafted field** is `sl remove-field <s>.<m>.<field>`. It refuses while a metric, a golden or a restriction names the field, listing each: change or remove those first. It adds its column to the model's `ignored_columns` in the same edit, or `sl status` would list it among the columns without a field and `next_step` would offer to draft it again. An approved field or join is removed only on the owner's yes, with `--force`.

**Deferring.** A table the owner leaves for later leaves scope (`sl scope --source <s> --exclude <table> --write`, naming the table as `sl tables` prints it and checking the preview lists it under Removed) and is named in the handoff; draft fields inside approved models do not block later phases and are named there too. Let the owner skip a column and come back, and stop whenever they want: the files hold the state. After each batch, `sl validate` and `sl status`.

**The same table in two sources.** The same table name may appear in two sources; models are named per source (`warehouse.orders`, `lake.orders`). Ask which one is the trusted one for each figure and say so in the descriptions.

## Review forms

A busy owner may answer a readback as a form instead of in chat. `sl review export --readback <readback_id> --format md` writes the pending readback, its numbered items and the open questions of the models it reads back, to `.sl/reviews/<readback_id>.md` (`--format json` for a tool). Hand the owner that file; they fill in a verdict (yes, no or change) and their words per item, and an answer per question. Then `sl review apply <file> --by "<owner>"` records each answer in the ledger as `sl reply` and `sl question resolve` would, refusing an item that changed since export; it never approves. For each recorded yes run what it names, `sl approve --target <ref>` or `sl goldens confirm --name <g>`; for a no or a change, make the typed edit and read the item back again. A form's verdict is the owner's only when they filled it in themselves: never fill one in for them. "Approve everything" approves nothing, however busy the owner is: say so, batch your questions into as few messages as you can, and send each batch's readback as one form.
